Europe’s Diesel Premium Is Back at $56

In two months the European diesel crack has halved, from $103.84 a barrel to $56.65, right on the $56 ABCD target. The squeeze is over, but the shortage isn’t: the gasoil curve is still in backwardation and stocks are still thin. Here is the snapshot, and how much of the drop has reached the pump.

Static reference: 26 November 2026 · ICE settlements of 25 November, Belgian maximum price valid from 26 November

The diesel crack is what refiners earn for turning a barrel of Brent into diesel. It is also what everyone who drives, farms, ships or heats with gasoil pays on top of crude. In September it was the most expensive it has been all year. On Wednesday it closed at $56.65: back at its 12-month average of $58.64, but still more than twice the pre-war level.

The $56 target

The fall from the September high has run in a measured way. Projecting the earlier $80 to $104 swing as an ABCD pattern gave $56 as the completion level. The crack closed at $56.65 on Wednesday and printed $57.55 on Thursday morning: the move has reached its target.

A completed pattern is a place to stop selling, not a guarantee of a turn. What happens next depends on whether the shortage underneath is still there, and the curve says it is.

Still in backwardation

When a market is short of barrels today, the front month trades above the next one: buyers pay up to have it now. The size of that gap is the cleanest daily read on how tight prompt diesel is in Europe.

So the crack has come back to earth faster than the shortage has eased. In June a $36 crack came with a nearly flat curve. Today’s $56 still comes with buyers paying almost $40 a tonne to have diesel now rather than in a month.

1,762 kt

Independent storage in Amsterdam–Rotterdam–Antwerp, week to 19 November, down 1.3%. In mid-October they stood at 1,782 kt against 2,255 kt a year earlier: roughly a fifth below last year.

Europe’s imports

803 kb/d

EU-27 and UK diesel and gasoil imports, November to date, against 816 kb/d in October. Steady, not rising: no flood of cargoes is on its way to rebuild stocks.

Where it comes from

India, Saudi Arabia

Early November imports into the ARA hub came mostly from India (39%) and Saudi Arabia (35%), with Canada third: long voyages, through the same sea lanes that drove the spring spike.

At the Belgian pump

Belgium caps the pump price. The FOD Economie sets a maximum for diesel B7 from ARA quotes and the euro–dollar rate; stations may sell below it, never above. Today it fell again.

Today’s €2.203 cap, taken apart. The diesel itself, at the 25 November ICE settlement and the ECB rate of $1.0935, is about €0.87 a litre. The rest is fixed: excise, levies and the distribution margin, then VAT on top of all of it.

Since the record cap of 28 October, the wholesale price of diesel has fallen by about €0.30 a litre, or €0.36 with VAT. The cap has fallen €0.307. So most of the drop has reached the pump, with about 5 cents a litre still to come if gasoil holds here. Two things keep the pump price high. More than half of every litre is tax, which doesn’t fall when oil does. And the euro is 2% weaker than in late October, which makes dollar-priced diesel dearer.

Confirm or cancel

The floor holds

Crack above $56, curve above $30/t

The target marks the low, and the remaining shortage keeps a premium in diesel through the winter heating season.

Normalisation

Below $50, curve under $20/t

The backwardation fades and the crack heads back toward June’s $36. That would be the first real relief at the pump since spring.

The squeeze returns

Back above $65–70

A cold snap, a refinery outage or new trouble in the shipping lanes. With stocks thin, there is little cushion.

What to watch

  1. The curve every evening: a crack that holds $56 while the curve narrows is healthy; a crack that holds while the curve widens again is a warning.
  2. Thursday ARA stocks from Insights Global: a rebuild toward 2 million tonnes would confirm the shortage is easing.
  3. The December gasoil expiry in mid-December: the roll to January will show how much the market pays to hold prompt barrels over year-end.
  4. The Belgian cap: about 5 cents of the wholesale fall still to pass through. Check it against the evening settlement on the Europe Diesel Pulse.
