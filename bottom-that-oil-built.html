The Bottom That Oil Built

Gold and silver made their low on Friday 30 October, the same day crude oil made its high. Oil was the instigator on the way down and the trigger on the way up. Here is the chain, the volatility around the US midterms, and the levels that confirm the bottom or cancel it.

Written 7 November 2026 · daily closes from the desk’s price log

For a month, every rise in crude was a reason to sell gold. Oil up meant inflation fear, inflation fear meant more Fed hikes priced, and hikes mean higher real yields and a stronger dollar: the two things gold likes least. When oil broke, the chain ran backwards.

From Friday's close to this Friday's: gold +5.1%, silver +5.7%, the gold miners (GDX) +13.6%, WTI −12.8%. Miners outrunning the metal more than two to one is what a turn usually looks like: equity buyers move first.

The mechanism

Oil → hikes → real yields

Not a law of nature: in a supply shock oil and gold often rise together. This time oil rose on Hormuz risk while the Fed was already leaning toward hiking, so crude fed straight into rate expectations.

When the metals trade the inverse of oil, the turn in the metals comes from the turn in oil. Watching gold alone, the 30 October low looked like capitulation. Watching oil, it was the top of a squeeze.

Volatility rose into the midterms

Bottoms are rarely quiet. In the two weeks around the US midterm elections, daily ranges widened across the chain, and most of all in oil, the market that was setting the pace.

High minus low, as a share of the previous close. Wider ranges at a low mean forced sellers meeting new buyers; the price stops trending and starts swinging. That is the bottoming process: not one day, but a zone that gets tested.

Confirm or cancel

Confirms the bottom

Gold closes above $4,147

Thursday's high. Then $4,200. With silver leading: the gold/silver ratio falling from 78.4 toward 75, and silver back above $53.48, Thursday's high.

Cancels it

A close below the lows

Gold below $3,870, silver below $48.62. Then 30 October was a pause, not a bottom.

The swing factor

Oil, again

If the Strait of Hormuz flares and crude climbs back toward $100, the chain runs forward again. A Fed hike on 9 December is about three-quarters priced; a hot US inflation print would add to it.

What to watch

  1. WTI around $90: the metals' fortunes are still tied to it. A move back above $100 is the main risk to the bottom.
  2. Tue 10 Nov, US CPI (October): still carries October's high oil. Judge it on core.
  3. The gold/silver ratio: a real bottom usually has silver leading out of it.
  4. Wed 9 Dec, the Fed, and the oil-to-real-yield link that drove all of this. See December Decides.
