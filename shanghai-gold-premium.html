What is the Shanghai gold premium?

The Shanghai Gold Exchange fixes a benchmark price in yuan per gram every afternoon at 14:15 Beijing time. Converted to dollars per ounce and compared with Western spot at the same moment, it shows what China pays for physical gold against the rest of the world.

Premium = SGE PM benchmark (yuan/g × 31.1035 ÷ USD/CNY) − Western spot

Why is there a premium at all?

Gold trades in three big pools: London, New York and Shanghai. Metal moves freely between London and New York, but into China it needs import permits from the central bank. That makes Shanghai the one pool where the local price can drift away from the world price.

When Chinese buyers want more gold than the permits let in, Shanghai trades at a premium. When they want less, or the yuan weakens, it slips to a discount. That makes the premium the cleanest daily read on Chinese physical demand, the largest single source of physical buying in the world.

What is the India gold premium?

India’s benchmark is the IBJA 999 gold rate, published twice a day per 10 grams. It includes India’s 15% import duty, which makes the headline premium look enormous: about $645 an ounce on 30 October. Taking the duty out leaves the real demand signal. Above zero, Indian buyers pay more than the world price even after duty; below zero, local dealers are discounting to move metal.

How to read the premium

The premium does not tell you whether gold goes up or down tomorrow. It tells you who is carrying the price: physical buyers in the East or investors in the West, over weeks and months, not days.

A premium is not “good” and a discount is not “bad”. Asian buyers are price-sensitive: they tend to buy the dips, which lifts the premium, and step back when gold rallies fast, which turns it into a discount. On the Desk the premium is confirmation, not a trigger: it says whether physical demand supports a view built on real rates and the dollar.
