Inflation or Deflation?

The Protection Map: what holds up in each of the four economic weathers, how a hedge fund and an individual build for all of them, and the ETFs to do it with from the US, Europe and Asia-Pacific.

Last checked 7 November 2026

Gold may be the core. It is not the house.

Almost everyone is exposed to one of two risks: that money loses its value faster than expected, or that the economy contracts and prices fall. Most portfolios are built for only one of them, usually without the owner knowing which. The question is not whether to protect your savings. It's what you are protecting them against.

Growth and inflation are the two forces that move almost every asset. Each can surprise up or down, which gives four weathers. Every asset has one weather it loves and one it can't survive.

The proof: 2008 and 2022

Two years, two opposite shocks. 2008 was a deflation bust; 2022 was an inflation shock. Look at what the same assets did in each.

2008 (deflation bust)2022 (inflation shock)

Swipe sideways to see the whole chart.

Long Treasuries were the best hedge of 2008 (+34%) and one of the worst assets of 2022 (−31%). Commodities did the exact opposite. Only two assets came through both years without a serious loss: cash and gold. That is why they are the core, and why everything else has to be chosen for the weather.

How a hedge fund does it, and how an individual can

The hedge fund

Bridgewater’s All Weather

Ray Dalio’s firm launched All Weather in 1996. The idea is to balance risk, not money: hold stocks, nominal bonds, inflation-linked bonds, gold and commodities so that each of the four weathers has something that thrives. Because bonds are less volatile, they are leveraged up with futures and swaps until they carry as much risk as stocks. Since 2025 a version trades as an ETF, ALLW.

The individual

Harry Browne’s Permanent Portfolio

Four equal parts, rebalanced once a year: stocks for prosperity, long-term Treasuries for deflation, gold for inflation, and cash (Treasury bills) for tight money and recession. No forecasting, no leverage, four funds.

Permanent Portfolio, 2022−12.5%
Both survived 2008 beautifully and both stumbled in 2022, for the same reason: they lean on long-dated bonds, and an inflation shock destroys long duration. The fix is not to drop bonds. It is to decide how long your bonds are by reading the weather, instead of by habit.

The Decision Framework way

  1. Hold a core that needs no forecast. Gold and cash came through both 2008 and 2022. They are the foundation, not the bet.
  2. Choose your duration by the weather. Short inflation-linked bonds and T-bills when the inflation tells light up; long government bonds only when the deflation tells do. Duration is the single decision that broke the classic portfolios in 2022.
  3. Add satellites for the weather you can see. Commodities and energy for an inflation boom, staples and long bonds for a bust, stocks and property for a disinflation boom.
  4. Read the tells, not the headlines. Real yields, breakeven inflation, crack spreads, the gold/silver ratio, the dollar and the Fed path move before the news calls the turn. Rebalance when the weather changes.

Where you live changes the answer

EU & EEA

UCITS only

Retail investors generally can't buy US-listed ETFs. Every sleeve above has a Europe-listed version. Watch the currency: a dollar TIPS or T-bill fund is also a bet on the dollar.

Belgium

Taxes reshape the menu

Gains on funds with more than 10% bonds pay the 30% Reynders tax. Since 1 January 2026 a 10% capital gains tax applies to most other gains above a €10,000 yearly exemption. Distributions pay 30% withholding, and every trade pays stock exchange tax. Check each fund with your broker before you buy.

United States

The full menu

Every fund here is available, including ALLW and PFIX. TIPS funds pass the inflation adjustment through as taxable income, so many hold them in tax-advantaged accounts.

United Kingdom

Long linkers, short memory

Index-linked gilts are among the longest bonds in the world. They were hit hard in 2022 despite paying inflation: in the UK, the inflation hedge is the short end.

Asia-Pacific

Local bonds, local cash

Singapore, Hong Kong and Australia list local government bond and cash funds. US-listed funds are usually reachable, with US withholding tax and estate-tax exposure for non-US investors; Irish UCITS funds avoid the estate-tax issue.

Everywhere

Physical is separate

An ETF is a claim on a fund. For metal in your own hands or your own name, see the dealer comparison and the Dealer Directory.
