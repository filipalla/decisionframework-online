Priced In, Not Over

The December rate hikes from the Fed, the ECB, the Bank of Japan and most likely the Bank of England are already in the price. What is not in the price is the pipeline behind them: producer prices running at 6% to 12% across the world. Here is what is priced, the two weeks to US CPI, the rest of the cycle, and what it means for gold.

Static reference: 26 November 2026 · market pricing after US PCE on 25 November

On Wednesday the Fed's favourite inflation gauge came in hot: core PCE 3.3% against 3.2% expected, the highest since November 2023. The market barely moved. That tells you where we are. The December hikes are done deals in the price; the question has moved to how far this cycle runs into 2027, and the answer sits in producer prices.

The pipeline gap: PPI against CPI

Consumer prices are what central banks target. Producer prices are what is coming. Right now producer prices run up to eight times faster.

Sweden belongs on this list too, at 11.8%. The cause is the same everywhere: the oil shock from the Iran war. Crude import prices have roughly doubled on the year in Sweden and are up 88% in the UK. Where the gap is widest and consumers are weak, as in China, companies eat the cost. Where demand holds, it reaches the shelves with a lag of months.

The December hikes respond to the inflation we have already seen. The pipeline is the inflation still on its way. That is why the hikes are priced but the cycle is not over.

Beyond December: the rest of the cycle

Every major central bank is hiking at once for the first time since 2022–23, and all of them started late: the ECB in June, the Fed in September, the Bank of England only this month. Markets see a short cycle, with the Fed peaking near 4.5% by mid-2027 and the last hikes in Europe and Japan by spring.

The risk to that picture is the pipeline. If producer prices of 6% to 12% reach consumers in the first half of 2027, the cycle runs longer. CME futures already put a 53% chance on the Fed going at least another 100 basis points by September 2027.

What it means for gold

Hot prints

Hikes priced further out

Real yields and the dollar rise: the headwind gold has traded under since October. The risk is not December but a January and March path the market adds.

In line

December delivered, guidance decides

Mostly priced. A 'one and done' tone from the Fed would ease real yields and help gold.

Cold prints

Hikes priced out

Real yields fall and gold gets relief. A Bank of Japan hold is the exception: a weaker yen lifts the dollar and caps it.

For gold the date that matters is not the last hike but the moment markets stop adding hikes. In the last cycle gold made its major low in November 2022, eight months before the Fed's final hike in July 2023. On today's pricing that moment falls in the first quarter of 2027.
