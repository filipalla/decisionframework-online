Next week · Tuesday 10 November, 14:30 Brussels (08:30 New York)

US consumer prices for October, four days after a strong jobs report and with euro-area inflation at 4.0%. September’s CPI came in at 3.7% against 3.6% expected and briefly lifted the odds of a Fed hike, before the 2-year yield rallied back overnight. Watch the same three needles on Tuesday: the 2-year yield, the dollar and gold. A hot number that the 2-year shrugs off again would say the market no longer fears a hike.

Why yields, the dollar and gold move together

An inflation number moves a market only through what it changes about interest rates. Hot inflation means a central bank has to stay tight; soft inflation lets it ease. The gap between those expectations moves currencies: when Europe runs hot and the US runs soft, the euro rises and the dollar falls. The dollar is the net sum of all those moves, the needle on the scale.

Watch three needles: the 2-year yield for what the Fed will do, the 10-year real yield for gold’s opportunity cost, and the dollar for the net verdict. Whatever does not show up in one of them shows up in another.

What the yield curve says

The 2s10s, the 10-year yield minus the 2-year, was deeply inverted in 2022 and 2023, the classic warning that markets expected rate cuts and slower growth. It is now positive again at +0.35 points. A curve that steepens because long yields rise, as now, says investors want more pay to hold long debt: a sign of inflation worry and heavy government borrowing rather than recession.

Credit stress: the early warning

The high-yield spread is what the riskiest US companies pay above Treasuries. When it widens, lenders pull back and leveraged positions are cut everywhere, metals and oil included. At 3.48% it sits just above calm. A move above 4% would be the first sign that the inflation story is turning into a deflationary squeeze.
