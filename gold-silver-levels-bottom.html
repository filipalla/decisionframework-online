Gold Jumps 4%, Silver 10%

The levels that decide whether the bottom is in. Precious metals just staged their strongest rebound in months, two days after the hottest core inflation print in three years. Here is what the bounce has repaired, and where the charts say it will be tested.

Static reference: Friday 27 November 2026, European morning · Charts made with Investing.com

Gold futures traded at $3,679 on Friday morning, up 4.1%. Silver jumped 10.3% to $44.18, platinum 9.1% and copper 5.3%. For a market that has shed more than $1,000 an ounce since late August, this is the first countermove with real force behind it.

Read those numbers with care. COMEX did not settle on Thursday because of Thanksgiving, so Friday’s futures change is measured from Wednesday and covers two sessions. Spot prices traded through the holiday and give the cleaner read: gold up about 3.2% on the day, silver about 7%. Either way, silver is moving more than twice as fast as gold.

The question is whether this is the low, or just a relief rally in a downtrend that has made lower highs since August. The answer is written on the charts, at a handful of very specific levels.

The bad news that stopped working

Wednesday should have been the final blow. Core PCE rose 3.3% year over year in October, a tenth above consensus and the highest reading since November 2023. Third-quarter GDP was revised up to 4.3%. Spot gold broke $3,500 and hit its lowest level since September 2025.

Then the selling dried up. A quarter-point hike at the December 8–9 FOMC meeting was already priced at more than 90%, and hot data could not make a near-certainty any more certain. The 10-year Treasury yield fell about 9 basis points after the release, and the dollar index slipped back from its 20-month high near 104.6.

That tells you what gold is really trading. Not the inflation print, but real yields and the dollar. If both keep easing, gold can recover even with a Fed that is still hiking.

The setup was stretched, too. Gold had fallen about 37% from its January record near $5,600 and traded some 20% below its 200-day moving average, the widest gap since June 2013. Its daily RSI touched 23. Markets that oversold do not need good news to bounce. They need bad news to stop working.

Gold: a bounce with walls still to climb

Since late October, gold’s four daily EMAs have been stacked in bearish order, the 9 below the 21 below the 55 below the 89, and every November bounce died at the 9-day line. Friday’s bar closed back above it at $3,664. That is a first repair, not a trend change.

The Fibonacci retracement of the November leg, from $4,161 to Wednesday’s $3,427 low, maps the road. The 38.2% level at $3,705 sits just overhead. The real test is $3,792–$3,798, where the 50% retracement meets the 21-day EMA. Relief rallies in a downtrend tend to die at overlaps like that. A daily close above it would change the picture.

Beyond it, the walls get thicker. The 61.8% level at $3,879 matches the $3,882 shelf where the mid-November breakdown accelerated. Then comes $4,000: the 78.6% retracement, the 55-day EMA at $4,003 and the floor that held gold all summer. Old support becomes resistance, and $4,000 is exactly where buyers gave up in November.

Momentum confirms it is early. The daily MACD (21, 55, 8) sits at −205.6, still below its signal line at −179.2. A bullish crossover would be the first sign that the selling pressure is truly spent.

The weekly chart shows how big the job is. Even with a 3.8% gain so far this week, gold at $3,671 is below the Tenkan-sen at $3,917, below the Kijun-sen at $4,137, and far beneath a cloud that stays above $4,500 into the new year.

The line that matters most is the lagging span. It plots this week’s close 26 weeks back, into late May and June, when gold held its floor near $4,000. Those candles now hang directly over it. Before the weekly trend can turn, price has to reclaim the levels it traded at half a year ago. That means $4,000, again.

When the daily and the weekly charts point at the same number, it deserves respect. A rally into $4,000 is a test, not a breakout.

Gold levels to watch

  • $3,664: the 9-day EMA, reclaimed on Friday
  • $3,705: the 38.2% retracement, first resistance
  • $3,792–$3,798: the 50% retracement and 21-day EMA, the key test
  • $3,880: the 61.8% retracement and the mid-November breakdown shelf
  • $4,000: the 78.6% retracement, 55-day EMA, summer floor and weekly overhang
  • $3,427: Wednesday’s 15-month low; a break below it cancels the bounce

Silver: the faster horse

Silver is where the rebound is loudest, and that matters more than the size of the move. It traded below $40 on Wednesday and was back above $44 on Friday. The gold/silver ratio fell from about 88 to 83 in two sessions.

When silver outruns gold at a low, buyers are taking risk, not hiding. The high-beta metal tends to turn first, which makes a falling ratio one of the better early signs of a metals bottom. It is not proof, but it is the kind of confirmation a bottom needs.

On the hourly chart, silver is drawing a potential inverse head and shoulders. The left shoulder is the $40.80 low on November 23, the head is Wednesday’s $37.99 low, and the right shoulder formed around $41 on Thursday while U.S. markets were shut. Friday’s rally carried price straight into the neckline at about $44.10, right on the 50% retracement of the decline from $50.27.

The short-term trend has already flipped. Price is above all four hourly EMAs, the 55 is crossing above the 89, and the hourly MACD is positive at 0.79 against a 0.53 signal line.

The measured move is striking. The head sits about $6.10 below the neckline, which projects a breakout to roughly $50.20, almost exactly the $50.27 high where the decline began. On the way up, the 61.8% retracement at $45.58, the 78.6% at $47.64 and the descending trendline from November 10, now near $48.20, are the hurdles.

That is a big call for an hourly pattern, so the rules matter more than the target. Until the neckline breaks, the pattern is only potential, and many fail right there. The trigger is an hourly close above the neckline, ideally on rising volume. The lower-risk entry usually comes afterward, on a pullback that holds the old neckline from above. A drop back under it voids the setup.

The daily Ichimoku chart shows where the real fight is. Friday’s 9.8% surge stopped dead at the Tenkan-sen at $43.99, the line that has capped every bounce since early November. The Kijun-sen at $47.42 is next, and the cloud starts far higher, near $54.

The lagging span, plotted 26 sessions back, lands in late October, when silver traded between $49 and $50. That is overhead supply, and it is exactly where the hourly target ends. When an hourly projection and a daily resistance zone land in the same place, that zone becomes the battleground.

Here is how I would map the best case. If the neckline breaks and momentum holds, silver can overshoot to the $52 area, where it moved sideways on November 11 and 12 before the slide. Silver rarely stops neatly at a target. From there, a pullback to about $49 would be the backtest of the broken trendline, the classic test of whether old resistance has turned into support. Hold $49, and the next leg up has a base. Lose the trendline again, and the November downtrend is back in charge.

Silver levels to watch

  • $43.99: the daily Tenkan-sen, the first roadblock
  • $44.10: the neckline and 50% retracement; an hourly close above it triggers the pattern
  • $47.42 and $48.20: the daily Kijun-sen and the descending trendline
  • $49–$50: the daily overhang and the measured-move target at $50.27
  • $52: the November 11–12 range, the stretch target
  • $37.99: the head; a break below it voids the setup

What a real bottom looks like

Gold rarely bottoms in a single day. Its last great base shows the template.

From 2016 to 2019, gold spent three years capped near $1,350–$1,365. It did not turn on its first low. It sank to about $1,165 in August 2018, recovered, and when it pulled back in May 2019, it held near $1,270, well above the old low. That higher low was the signal. The breakout came in June 2019, when price cleared the ceiling and the weekly cloud at the same time, and within three months gold was trading above $1,550.

The Ichimoku view of that base mirrors today’s chart, and that is the point. In 2019, the lagging span first had to climb back over the prices of 26 weeks earlier before the trend could run. Today, gold faces the same test at $4,000. The edge in 2019 came from waiting for the higher low, with risk defined just below it, rather than buying the first bounce off the bottom.

Today, Friday is the first bounce. If this is a bottom, the better entry comes on the retest: a pullback that holds above $3,427 in gold while silver keeps leading. Buy there, and you know exactly where you are wrong.

The bottom line

Friday’s rebound is real, but it is one day. Gold has repaired nothing structural yet. Silver has started to.

Confirms the bottom

Gold above $3,798

A daily close above the $3,792–$3,798 zone that holds on a pullback, with the daily MACD crossing up.

Confirms it too

A higher low, silver leading

A retest that holds above $3,427, silver through its $44.10 neckline and the gold/silver ratio still falling.

Cancels it

Back below $3,500 or $40

A daily close back below $3,500 in gold or $40 in silver, the ratio back toward 88, or a break of Wednesday’s lows.

The calendar will decide fast. The Fed meets on December 8–9 with a hike all but priced in, so its guidance on January and 2027 matters more than the decision. November CPI follows on December 10. If gold holds its ground through a hike and a hot print, the market will have shown that the bad news is finally in the price. That is how bottoms are usually confirmed: not by good news, but by bad news that stops working.

These levels will be tested within days, and the Fed on December 9 and CPI on December 10 will deliver the verdict.
