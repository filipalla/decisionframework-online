America’s Diesel Margin Breaks Its Uptrend

The US diesel crack has closed below the line that held all of 2026, from the January low through the October low. Pump diesel is 83 cents off its September record, stocks are rebuilding from a low base, and the futures are already pointing lower. Here is the snapshot, and what it means for inflation and gold.

Static reference: 26 November 2026 · EIA spot to 24 November, futures to 25 November, pump price 23 November

The diesel crack is what US refiners earn for turning a barrel of crude into diesel, and what every trucker, farmer and heating-oil household pays on top of oil. It peaked at $105.91 a barrel on 20 October. On Tuesday it closed at $67.51: still twice the pre-war level, but below the 2026 uptrend for the first time.

The break

The trendline runs through the two lows of the year: $25.08 on 14 January and $60.80 on 2 October. It held through the spring sell-off and the October flush. Tuesday’s close at $67.51 is the first finish below it.

Confirms the break

A close below $60.80

The first lower low since January. The futures crack is already there.

Cancels it

Back above the line, about $68–70

A cold snap in the Northeast, a refinery outage or new trouble at Hormuz would do it.

The rest of the barrel

Gasoline went first

The futures gasoline crack is $17.73 and the 3-2-1 crack $35.61. Diesel was the last leg holding the refining margin up.

Stocks: rebuilding from a low base

Distillate stocks have risen for three weeks, from a 99.3 million barrel low on 30 October to 105.6 million on 20 November. That is the relief the margin is pricing. They are still about 6.6 million barrels below the same week last year and roughly 7% under the 2021–2025 average.

Refiners are running hard, at about 96% of capacity in mid-November, and pushed planned maintenance from 2026 into 2027 to capture the margins. A lower crack makes that maintenance cheaper to take, and every unit that goes down is diesel that is not made. Thin stocks plus a refinery fleet that has been run flat out is why the break can still fail.

About seven-tenths of the wholesale drop has reached the pump so far. The gap between pump and futures has widened by 33 cents, which is what is still to come if futures hold here. Prices rise like rockets and fall like feathers: in Belgium, where the pump price is capped by formula, most of the drop passed through within weeks.

The spread across the country is wide: about $7.44 a gallon in California against $5.29 on the Gulf Coast. The EIA’s own outlook has retail diesel averaging $5.25 in 2026 and $5.03 in 2027.

America and Europe: the same move

Europe’s diesel crack has made the same round trip: from $103.84 in September to $56.65 on Wednesday, right on its $56 target. Both margins have roughly halved from peaks above $100. The difference is in the curve: European gasoil is still in backwardation, so prompt diesel there remains scarce. See Europe’s Diesel Premium Is Back at $56.

Why it matters for inflation and gold

Diesel moves freight, food and farm costs, so a breaking diesel margin is one of the first places producer-price pressure eases. That is the counterweight to the 6% to 12% producer prices in Priced In, Not Over: if the break holds, less of that pipeline reaches consumers in the first half of 2027, the case for more rate hikes weakens, and real yields have less reason to rise. For gold, that is the friendlier path.

A diesel margin below its uptrend leans toward disinflation. A failed break, back above the line on a cold winter, leans the other way. The October low at $60.80 decides which.

What to watch

  1. The EIA spot crack against $60.80: a close below confirms the first lower low of the year.
  2. Wednesday’s EIA weekly stocks: a fourth build would back the break; a draw in early winter would challenge it.
  3. Monday’s pump survey: about 33 cents of the wholesale fall still to pass through.
  4. Refinery maintenance and the weather: outages and a cold Northeast are the two ways the margin bounces.
