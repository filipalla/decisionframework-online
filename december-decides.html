What is already in the price

Fed hike on 9 December
76.8%

CME FedWatch, after the October jobs report (Friday 6 November): payrolls +165,000, about 2.5 times the forecast. Logged event by event on the desk’s hike tracker.

ECB hike on 17 December
72%

Market pricing reported on 6 November, after euro-area inflation rose to 4.0% in October. Core is only 2.5%; energy is up 20.8% on the year.

With both near three in four, a hike is the expected case for both. The surprise, and the bigger move, is a hold.

Four outcomes

How markets tend to react. The dollar and gold in dollars move on the gap between the Fed and the ECB; gold in euros moves on gold and the euro together, so a weaker euro cushions a European holder.

The energy trap

CPI measures a month’s average prices. October’s CPI on Tuesday 10 November still carries October’s high oil; November’s CPI on 10 December is where this month’s oil drop finally shows, so a soft headline is likely. If the Strait of Hormuz flares again before then, that soft number arrives in a new war: a “disinflation” print just as energy turns up again.

Euro-area inflation is energy-led, US inflation is hot underneath. Lower oil weakens the ECB’s case for a hike more than the Fed’s. A renewed oil spike does the opposite, and makes the ECB the bigger hawkish surprise.

What to watch, in order

  1. Tue 10 Nov, US CPI (October): judge it on core, not the energy-heavy headline.
  2. Fri 4 Dec, US jobs (November): the last big input before the Fed. Watch the 2-year.
  3. Wed 9 Dec, the Fed: the decision, then the dots: they move the 2-year; the tone on inflation moves the long end.
  4. Thu 10 Dec, US CPI (November): confirms the Fed or makes it look a day late.
  5. Thu 17 Dec, the ECB: having seen all of the above, with new projections.
