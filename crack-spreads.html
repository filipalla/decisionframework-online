What is a crack spread?

Nobody puts crude oil in a truck. It has to be refined, and the crack spread is what the refiner earns for doing that: the price of the fuel minus the price of the crude it came from. The name comes from the process itself, “cracking” heavy crude molecules into lighter fuels.

A wide crack spread means fuel is scarce relative to crude: refiners can sell everything they make at a large margin. A narrow one means there is plenty of fuel, or that demand is weak.

How are crack spreads calculated?

Fuel is quoted in dollars per gallon and crude in dollars per barrel, so the fuel price is multiplied by 42, the number of gallons in a barrel. This page uses three measures:

Diesel crack   = diesel × 42 − Brent
Gasoline crack = gasoline × 42 − WTI
3-2-1 crack    = ((2 × gasoline + 1 × diesel) × 42 − 3 × WTI) ÷ 3

The 3-2-1 crack is the industry’s rough model of a typical refinery: three barrels of crude in, two barrels of gasoline and one of diesel out. It shows the margin of refining as a whole, where the separate diesel and gasoline cracks show where the pressure sits.

Why is the diesel crack so much higher than gasoline?

Diesel moves freight, tractors, ships and industry, and heats homes in winter. A refinery cannot simply switch its output from gasoline to diesel, so when diesel is short, its margin can run far ahead. Gasoline demand peaks in the summer driving season and fades from September.

That gives both cracks a season. A gasoline crack that stays high after the summer, or a diesel crack that climbs before winter has begun, points to a supply problem rather than the calendar. Today’s picture, diesel near crisis levels while gasoline sits at normal margins, is the supply kind.

What does a high diesel crack mean for inflation?

Almost every physical good travels on diesel at some point. When the diesel crack stays high, transport and food costs rise even on days when crude oil falls. Europe runs far more on diesel than the US, which makes the diesel crack one of the main channels through which an energy shock keeps euro-area inflation sticky and the ECB under pressure.

Watch the crack, not only the crude. Crude falling while the diesel crack stays high means inflation is still in the pipeline. The crack rolling over while crude holds means the squeeze is easing, and fuel prices should follow within weeks.

Why it matters for gold and interest rates

The diesel crack is one of the indicators that answers the bigger question behind this site: are we protecting money against inflation or against deflation? Sticky fuel inflation pushes central banks to hold rates high. Whether that helps or hurts gold depends on what real yields do next, which is why the crack sits on the Desk next to real rates and the dollar.
